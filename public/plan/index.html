The concept

A room in orbit made mostly of windows.

HCSA is a design for a facility in low Earth orbit for short visits. Nobody lives there; people come to look at Earth, move around, meet, do research and spend time among plants.

Its wall is 32 flat panels in the shape of a football. The 20 hexagons are glass: they are the windows. The 12 pentagons are solid: they house the solar panels and the shields that fold out over the windows around them. Every window borders three pentagons, so a shield can reach every one.

Inside is air at normal pressure and outside is vacuum, so the air pushes out on every panel with about 1.33 meganewtons on each hexagon. Holding that at the seams, and through the glass, is what this fall's work is about.

  • 11.15 m across
  • 2.25 m edges
  • 630 m³ inside
  • 32 panels
  • 90 seams
  • 1 atm inside, vacuum outside

Where we are

The design is simple enough to build.

  • The panel joint is redesigned to be simple (Rev S): two aluminium frames bolted face to face, one row of bolts every 75 mm, two O-rings and a sealant bead.
  • The whole shell is modelled in CAD: 32 panels, 90 seams, 2,610 bolts and every seal, with no parts overlapping.
  • The tests are designed, with drawings for every test piece and rig, and the test plan is going to Brown engineering faculty for review.
  • Every drawing, with dimensions and a plain explanation, is on the blueprints page.

The tests, smallest first

Each test answers one question.

T0: how strong is our glass?

Glass discs 50 mm across and 3 mm thick, pressed in the middle until they break. 30 or more per type of glass, so we know the real strength and how much it scatters.

October, in a small load frame.

Drawings and dimensions

T1: does the seam hold when it is pulled apart?

Full-size slices of the seam, 75, 150 and 225 mm long, pulled in a 100 kN load frame while we check the seal between the O-rings. Plus a 550 mm piece of the seam to check it goes together and holds air.

Parts ordered after the 8 November release; tests as soon as they arrive.

Drawings and dimensions

T2: how does a window panel bend?

A hexagon panel at quarter scale, 1.1 m across, with aluminium in place of glass, loaded with water up to 2 atm in a steel tub. It shows how the panes bend onto the frame and ribs.

Spring 2027.

Drawings and dimensions

FEA: how does the whole shell share the load?

Computer models of one panel, a cap of seven panels and the full shell. They tell us how much load the seams and corners really carry, which sets the bolt size.

October and November.
